Structure

What each layer means

◎

Wormhole

The single logical ingress gateway at the Galaxy boundary. It accepts a Mission and creates an explainable Trajectory without granting additional authority.

✹

Galaxy

The top-level ownership and isolation boundary. It connects directly to the Circles belonging to one person or organization.

★

Rogue Star

A shared external resource that belongs to no Galaxy. Multiple Galaxies may access it through explicit, governed connections; Mac Gemma is the current example.

✶

Constellation

A connected operational subgraph drawn from selected parts of one or more Circles. It is an overlay, not a routing or ownership layer.

◌

Circle

A Galaxy-level responsibility or domain grouping such as Home, Energy, or Satellite Communications. It contains Planets and Components.

●

Planet

The user-facing name for an autonomous Agent inside a Circle. Every Planet is classified as either Rocky or Giant.

R

Rocky Planet

A specialist Agent with a narrow domain or task Charter, such as RF link-budget analysis or pantry planning.

G

Giant Planet

A non-specialist Agent with a broad Charter that coordinates varied routine work and delegates deep expertise when needed.

◇

Circle Member

The umbrella term for anything assigned to a Circle: a Rocky Planet, Giant Planet, or Component.

C

Component

A non-agent Circle Member such as a capability, Satellite, Vault, Playbook, Gate, model, or service.

S

Satellite

A callable tool Component that gives a Planet one bounded operation. It executes when invoked but does not independently reason or own a Mission.

☼

Solar System

A Planet-centered view of its directly connected Satellites, capabilities, Playbooks, memories, policies, models, and services.

Circle ≠ ConstellationA Circle groups a domain. A Constellation connects selected parts of one or more Circles.
Rocky ≠ GiantBoth are Planets. A Rocky Planet is specialist; a Giant Planet is broad and non-specialist.
Planet ≠ ComponentA Planet decides and acts under a Charter. A Component enables or constrains that work.
Planet ≠ SatelliteA Planet reasons and owns work. A Satellite is a bounded tool invoked by an authorized Planet.
Galaxy ≠ Rogue StarA Galaxy owns its internal system. A Rogue Star is external and may be shared across Galaxies.

Direct connections define the view

The Solar System is computed from graph Orbits. It is not another agent or process.

Shared components create overlap

Two Planets can share the same Vault, Playbook, model, or policy while keeping separate Charters.

Authority stays bounded

Planning a purchase may be allowed, while placing the order still stops at the Purchase Gate.

Choosing the right term

Which term fits?

“Group all energy-related roles.”Use a Circle

Energy is a domain grouping, even when its members have different Charters and Satellites.

“Connect selected Home and Life Admin members for household planning.”Use a Constellation

The workflow is a connected subgraph spanning parts of two Circles.

“Perform an RF link-budget review.”Use a Rocky Planet

The task needs narrow specialist expertise and one accountable Charter.

“Coordinate whatever routine work arrives today.”Use a Giant Planet

The role needs a broad Charter and can delegate narrow work to Rocky Planets.

“Attach a shared calculation function.”Use a Satellite

It is a callable tool Component that can orbit one or more Planets without becoming an agent.

“Share one externally hosted model across several Galaxies.”Use a Rogue Star

The resource stays outside every Galaxy and is reached through governed Orbits.

“Combine scientific, market, and regulatory perspectives for one decision.”Use a Crew

Several Planets collaborate temporarily while retaining individual accountability.

Work and execution

How the Galaxy gets work done

Mission

A user goal submitted to the Galaxy.

Trajectory

The explainable route from Wormhole through Galaxy and Circle to the selected Planet.

Crew

A temporary set of Rocky and/or Giant Planets collaborating on one Mission.

Work Order

A bounded delegation carrying scope, effects, memory boundary, approvals, and trace identifiers.

Flight Plan

The selected Playbook and ordered steps used to execute a Work Order.

Gate

A policy or human-approval boundary before a protected action.

Graph and operations

How the system is governed and observed

Orbit

A typed graph relationship such as member of, can use, reads, governs, dispatches to, or accesses external.

Roster

The registered set of available Rocky and Giant Planet Charters.

Vault

An explicit, scoped, and auditable memory or data-isolation boundary.

Observatory

The monitoring and replay capability. Control Room is the product-facing interface.

Mission Log

The correlated event history for routing, Planets, models, Satellite invocations, approvals, and outcomes.

Charter

The declarative contract defining a Planet’s purpose, permissions, memory scopes, and lifecycle.
